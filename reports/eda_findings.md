# EDA Findings

Generated by `src/eda.py` against the 1041-case cleaned dataset (`data/processed/clean_cases.csv`). Every figure and number below comes directly from that file.

## Outcome distribution

Only 571 of 1041 cases (54.9%) have an outcome the extraction pipeline could actually find in the judgment text; the rest are genuinely missing and are excluded here (not counted as a fake 'unknown' outcome). Among the labeled cases:

- **set aside**: 509 (89.1% of labeled cases)
- **appeal allowed**: 30 (5.3% of labeled cases)
- **appeal dismissed**: 28 (4.9% of labeled cases)
- **petition dismissed**: 3 (0.5% of labeled cases)
- **leave to appeal granted**: 1 (0.2% of labeled cases)

'Set aside' dominates (509 cases) — consistent with a Supreme Court appellate docket, where a large share of the matters that reach a full hearing result in the lower court's order being set aside and remanded, rather than a flat dismissal.

## Cases per year

Case counts run from 1974 to 2025, peaking in 2024 with 124 cases. Coverage is uneven in earlier years — this is a corpus-composition artifact of what was available to collect, not a claim about actual Supreme Court caseload over time.

## Case type

425 of 1041 cases (40.8%) could not be classified — there was no `case_no` text for the classifier to read, not a guess forced into a bucket. Among the rest, Civil Petition is the largest group (299 cases).

## Judgment length

Word count: mean 3175, median 2178, max 53185. The distribution is right-skewed — most judgments are a few thousand words, with a long tail of much longer, more complex rulings.

## Most-cited sections

Section numbers are extracted without their governing statute, so `302` could mean PPC s.302 (murder) in one case and a different act's s.302 in another — this chart describes *which numbers recur*, not confirmed legal categories. The most frequent: 2 (407 cases), 4 (400 cases), 3 (378 cases), 5 (327 cases), 1 (285 cases).

## Bench size

A reliable bench size is only available for 22 of 1041 cases (see the data quality report for why). Among those: median 2, largest 10.
